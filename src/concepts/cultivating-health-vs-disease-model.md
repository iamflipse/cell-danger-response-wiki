# Cultivating Health vs. Disease-Centered Medicine

Reflecting on [Naviaux's Six Cardinal Elements](health-cycle-six-elements.md), **A Midwestern Doctor** highlights a foundational blind spot in modern healthcare: medicine is organized around naming and suppressing diseases rather than cultivating the underlying generators of health.

> "Medicine typically focuses on identifying and 'treating' diseases rather than identifying what creates health and cultivating that. Although the disease-focused model can often seem very impressive (and generate a lot of business from each identified 'disease'), without cultivating the core generators of health, complex chronic diseases will continually arise that cannot be fixed within the disease-centered model."
> — **A Midwestern Doctor**, [*Systemic Treatments for the Cell Danger Response*](https://www.midwesterndoctor.com/p/systemic-treatments-of-the-cell-danger)

## Two Competing Paradigms

| Dimension | Disease-Centered Model | Health-Cultivating / Salugenic Model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Core assumption** | Single cause → single effect; identify a distinct disease label and target it directly | **Pluricausal thresholds:** accumulated stressors tip cells into chronic defense; recovery requires systemic safety |
| **View of symptoms** | Broken machinery or isolated organ defect to suppress (or labeled "psychosomatic" if tests look normal) | Tissue-specific expressions of an unresolved [Cell Danger Response](what-is-cdr.md), or **physiological compensations** protecting vulnerable cells |
| **Primary levers** | Pharmaceuticals, symptom suppression, hormone/stimulant replacement | [Six cardinal elements](health-cycle-six-elements.md), removing active danger triggers, calming sympathetic over-activation, and targeted [induction bridges](clinical-practice-principles.md) |
| **Economic incentive** | Rewarded per named disease and ongoing symptom management | Low commercializability; emphasizes self-sustaining capacity that reduces lifelong dependency |

## Why the Disease Model Stumbles on Complex Chronic Illness

### 1. Same Label, Different Trigger Stack
Because the CDR activates once a person's **cumulative burden** (sub-toxic chemicals, infections, trauma, disrupted sleep/rhythms) crosses a threshold, two patients with identical diagnoses (e.g., ME/CFS, long-COVID, autoimmunity) may have arrived via completely different trigger histories. Conversely, the same environmental stressor can produce different symptoms depending on **which tissue** remained trapped in the CDR.

### 2. The "Psychosomatic" Trap
When a condition spans hormones, nerves, immunity, fatigue, and mood simultaneously, single-cause diagnostics come back inconclusive. As the essay notes, sufferers are frequently dismissed as having "psychosomatic" illness—made worse by a two-way biological loop:
- An active CDR frequently generates neuroinflammation, **depression, and anxiety** directly.
- Negative emotions and perceived danger signal the brain to reinforce the CDR, blocking the calm safety signals needed to exit it.

### 3. Partial Relief From Single Healthy Habits
In a threshold/systems model, changing one lever (taking a supplement, sleeping slightly better, reducing one stressor) often helps *partially* without resolving the illness—explaining why patients cycle through dozens of interventions and still relapse.

### 4. Mistaking Compensations for Defects
Clinicians quoted in the essay stress that many abnormal markers in chronic illness (such as [downregulated mitochondrial output or altered hormone response](endocrine-metabolism-diet.md)) are **deliberate protective adaptations**. Forcing energy production or pushing hormones onto unresponsive CDR tissue often causes dependency or iatrogenic flares instead of healing.
